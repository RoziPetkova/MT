## 1. The thesis in one paragraph

Gaze cueing (GC) is a robust, largely automatic attentional effect that is nonetheless modulated by face,
relationship and observer factors — including internal states (social exclusion, power priming). Sense of agency
(SoA) is known to reshape perception and attention for non-social events (intentional binding, sensory
attenuation, action-based attention), and people can feel agency over social outcomes, including another's gaze.
Prior work has only tested **gaze → agency** (Stephenson et al., 2018; Ulloa et al., 2019; Gutzeit et al., 2024).
This thesis tests the reverse, **agency → gaze cueing**: participants choose the avatar's gaze direction, and the
gaze either follows the choice (consistent), opposes it (inconsistent) or is chosen by the computer (control).
Three experiments vary only the direction-selection modality: **keypress (Exp1, N = 41) → mouse click (Exp2,
N = 48) → touchscreen tap (Exp3, N = 49)**; combined N = 138.

**Research questions** (`2.structure.md`)
- **RQ1** Does action–outcome consistency (AOC) modulate the GCE? → 2 × 3 rANOVA per experiment
- **RQ2** Does a larger range of movement strengthen that modulation? → mixed ANOVA with Experiment between
- **RQ3** Does AOC produce matching differences in explicit SoA? → Likert ratings (caused / controlled / predicted)

## 3. The argument chain of Chapter 1 (as currently drafted)

**Part I — GC is special and automatic**
1. Gaze is a privileged social signal: shared with primates (Emery 2000; Shepherd et al. 2006) but human eye
   morphology (white sclera) makes it uniquely readable (Kobayashi & Kohshima 1997; Tomasello et al. 2007).
2. Dedicated neural machinery (STS — Hoffman & Haxby 2000; Pelphrey et al. 2003; fast subcortical route — Senju &
   Johnson 2009) and very early development (Farroni et al. 2002; Batki et al. 2000; Reid & Striano 2005).
3. Gaze is the gateway to joint attention and ToM (Baron-Cohen 1995 — EDD; Tomasello et al. 2005).
4. Second-person neuroscience motivates moving from passive observer to active agent (Schilbach et al. 2013;
   Pfeiffer et al. 2013) — **the thesis's framing hook.**
5. Posner (1980) paradigm → gaze version (Friesen & Kingstone 1998; Driver et al. 1999; Langton & Bruce 1999).
   GCE typically 10–20 ms (Frischen et al. 2007; McKay et al. 2021).
6. Automaticity: works with non-predictive and counter-predictive gaze (Friesen & Kingstone 1998; Kuhn & Kingstone
   2009; Tipples 2008), under masking (Sato et al. 2007), and grows under load (Gregory & Jackson 2021);
   exogenous-like time course (gone at ~1 s SOA).
7. Evidence-accumulation reframing: GC may reflect processing efficiency at the cued location (Alister et al. 2024).

**Part II — but GC is modulable** (Dalmaso et al. 2020; McKay et al. 2021)
- *Face:* emotion (mixed; fear ↑ esp. in anxious observers), dominance, status, attractiveness.
- *Relationship:* familiarity, race/political in-group, trust (mixed).
- *Observer, stable:* gender, age, anxiety (Talipski et al. 2021 null), autistic traits, personality, politics.
- *Observer, temporary state:* rejection/exclusion (Wilkowski et al. 2009 vs. Capellini et al. 2019),
  **power priming (Cui et al. 2014) — the closest precedent: a feeling of (lacking) control changes GC.**
- Design choices justified from this literature: discrimination task, dynamic gaze, non-predictive gaze,
  neutral Caucasian face, SOA 210 ms.

**Part III — SoA**
- Definition (Haggard & Tsakiris 2009); intention–action–effect (Wen & Imamizu 2022); SoA ≠ SoO (Braun et al. 2018).
- Four models: comparator (Frith, Blakemore & Wolpert 2000), retrospective/Wegner (2002), cue integration
  (Synofzik et al. 2008; Moore & Fletcher 2012), predictive processing (Friston 2010; Apps & Tsakiris 2014).
- Classifications: FoA vs JoA (Synofzik et al. 2008); prospective vs retrospective (Chambon & Haggard 2012).
- **Mapping onto the design:** RT/GCE ≈ implicit (FoA); Likert ≈ explicit (JoA); initiation time ≈ prospective
  component; AOC ≈ retrospective component / Wegner's consistency criterion.
- Range of motion (H3): more movement = richer proprioceptive signals (↑ SoA) **vs.** noisier signals (↓ SoA,
  cue-integration reliability argument). Movement matters (Moore et al. 2009; Antusch et al. 2021;
  Krugwasser et al. 2019; David et al. 2016 vs Zama et al. 2017), but amplitude itself is unstudied → gap.
- Measurement: explicit vs implicit/intentional binding; dissociations (Wen et al. 2015; Grynszpan et al. 2019);
  binding critique (Reddy 2022) → why Likert was used.
- Mechanism: action–effect binding / ideomotor theory (Hommel et al. 2001).

**Part IV — agency changes perception/attention**
- Intentional binding (Haggard et al. 2002; Imaizumi & Tanno 2019; Beck et al. 2017).
- Sensory attenuation (Blakemore et al. 1999; Brown et al. 2013; Han et al. 2021 — attenuation without movement).
- Action-based attention & eye–hand coupling (Wen & Imamizu 2022; Johansson et al. 2001; Huang et al. 2012).
- Agency over social outcomes / others' gaze (Stephenson et al. 2018; Wegner et al. 2004; Grynszpan et al. 2019).
- **Gap:** gaze → agency is studied; agency → GC is not.

## 4. What the data say (from the audited results files)

**RT — per experiment** (`_02.4.cross_experiment_summary_RAW.md` Part 1)

| | Exp1 keypress | Exp2 mouse | Exp3 touch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaze main effect | sig. (η²ₚ = .263) | sig. (.270) | sig. (.222) |
| Action main effect | sig. (.119) | sig. (.220) | sig. (.076) |
| **Gaze × Action** | **n.s.** (p = .341) | **sig.** (η²ₚ = .206) | **sig.** (η²ₚ = .219) |
| GCE consistent | 15.0 ms * | 27.9 ms * | 25.1 ms * |
| GCE control | 14.1 ms * | 12.9 ms * | 18.9 ms * |
| GCE inconsistent | 5.0 ms n.s. | −10.8 ms n.s. (p = .070) | −12.8 ms n.s. (p = .056) |

**RT — combined (N = 138)** (`_02.4.rt_cross_experiment_results_RAW.md`)
- Raw-RT 3-way Gaze × Action × Experiment: p = .061 (marginal).
- Per-subject Cohen's *d* analysis: d × Experiment p = .015 (η²ₚ = .046); consistent > control > inconsistent;
  Experiment's simple effect significant only within **inconsistent** (p = .016); no pairwise experiment
  difference survives (see §6.1).
- No overall Experiment effect on RT, GCE size, or overall SoA ratings.

**Questionnaire** (`_02.4.q_data_cross_experiment_results_RAW.md`)
- Composite: consistent > inconsistent > control in all experiments (AC η²ₚ = .518).
- AC × Experiment sig. — driven by the **consistent** condition: Exp1 rated higher than Exp2 and Exp3; Exp2 = Exp3.
- *Caused* is the item most sensitive to modality; *controlled* and *predicted* carry the (only) link to the implicit
  effect: inconsistent-condition composite ↔ *d*, r = −.20 (predicted −.25, controlled −.19, caused n.s.).

**Headline interpretation in progress (General Discussion):** when the self-generated prediction conflicts with
the gaze (inconsistent), the GCE becomes unreliable and — with mouse/touch — reverses; keypress keeps gaze and
action independent. Explicit SoA is *highest* with the smallest movement (keypress), against the "more movement
→ more agency" intuition (candidate explanations in `Onwoing_work.md`: fluency of action selection — Chambon &
Haggard 2012; Chambon, Sidarus & Haggard 2014; cue-integration noise; effort only matters under unreliable
feedback — Minohara et al. 2016; keypress rated higher than touch/on-skin — Bergström-Lehtovirta et al. 2018).
