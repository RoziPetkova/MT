# Cross-Experiment RT Analysis — Results

[Draft. Sits structurally between the per-experiment results (Experiment 3) and the General Discussion's interpretive synthesis — this is the pure statistical reporting for the combined/cross-experiment tests, analogous to the Results section of each individual experiment chapter.]

Having in mind that the three experiments differ in the modality of the movement for direction selection - how the direction-selection response was made (keypress, mouse click, touchscreen tap) - and in order to investigate whether this difference is visible in RT (will the modality of the movement make any changes in RT), the data from all 3 experiments were also combined into a single cross-experiment analysis, with `Experiment` entered as a between-subjects factor (N = 41, 48, 49 for Experiments 1–3; N = 138 total). These tests were conducted in order to check whether response modality changes how gaze congruency and action consistency interact.

## Mixed ANOVA (raw RT)

To determine whether response action modality influenced the relationship between action-outcome consistency and gaze cueing, we first performed a mixed 2 (Gaze Congruency) × 3 (Action Consistency) × 3 (Experiment as between factor) mixed ANOVA on raw reaction times, Greenhouse–Geisser corrected throughout. The results showed that the three-way interaction — the direct test of whether response modality changes the relationship between gaze congruency and action consistency — approaches but does not reach conventional significance - F(3.76,253.95)=2.33,p=.061.

| Term | Result |
|---|---|
| Experiment (between) | F(2,135)=0.06, p=.939 (n.s.) |
| Gaze | F(1,135)=44.74, p<.001, η²ₚ=.249 |
| Gaze × Experiment | F(2,135)=0.06, p=.939 (n.s.) |
| Action | F(1.41,190.79)=20.20, p<.001, η²ₚ=.130 |
| Action × Experiment | F(2.83,190.79)=1.24, p=.298 (n.s.) |
| Gaze × Action | F(1.88,253.95)=22.00, p<.001, η²ₚ=.140 |
| **Gaze × Action × Experiment** | F(3.76,253.95)=2.33, **p=.061** (marginal) |

However, a significant simple main effect of gaze congruency was present in all 3 experiments - showing that the procedure worked correctly and all experiments were able to replicate the standard GC paradigm and to induce a GCE. The simple gaze effect, pooled across all 138 subjects and collapsed across Experiment, is confirmed significant within consistent (MD=22.68 ms, t(135)=8.44, p<.001) and control (MD=15.31 ms, t(135)=5.21, p<.001) — but does not reach significance in inconsistent conditions (MD=6.19 ms, t(135)=1.78, p=.078 uncorrected / p=1.000 Bonferroni-corrected). Confirms what was already observed in the single experiment analysis - inconsistent's GC effect isn't just weaker, it doesn't survive to reach significance in inconsistent trials. To investigate further, we conducted post hoc tests.

A post hoc on action confirmed that active conditions (cons and incons) are different than the passive (ctrl) one, both being faster than it (16.86 ms, t(135)=4.77, p_holm<.001, and 17.24 ms, t(135)=4.81, p_holm<.001, respectively). But did not differ between themselves (0.37 ms diff, t(135)=0.20, p_holm=.838).

A Post hoc on gaze x action showed that across the three experiments:
•	Consistent condition: Congruent RT is significantly faster than incongruent RT (MD=22.68 ms, t(135)=8.44, p<.001 — gaze cueing present).
•	Control condition: Congruent RT is significantly faster than incongruent RT (MD=15.31 ms, t(135)=5.21, p<.001 — gaze cueing present).
•	Inconsistent condition: Congruent RT is not significantly different from incongruent RT (MD=6.19 ms, t(135)=1.78, p=.078 uncorrected / p=1.000 Bonferroni — no reliable gaze cueing overall).

It also shows some action differences within each gaze level:
•	For congruent trials, consistent is faster than both inconsistent (MD=14.05 ms, t(135)=4.85, p<.001) and control (MD=20.55 ms, t(135)=4.96, p<.001); inconsistent trends faster than control too, but this does not reach significance (MD=6.49 ms, t(135)=1.51, p=1.000, n.s.).
•	For incongruent trials, the pattern flips: inconsistent is the fastest condition — faster than both consistent (MD=14.81 ms, t(135)=4.60, p<.001) and control (MD=27.99 ms, t(135)=6.55, p<.001); consistent is also faster than control (MD=13.18 ms, t(135)=3.34, p=.017).

## Log-transformed RT

The same 2 × 3 × 3 design was re-run on log(RT) (mean of the per-trial log, not the log of the mean), to address the possibility that raw milliseconds carry unequal variance across response modalities (experiments).

| Term | Result |
|---|---|
| **Gaze × Action × Experiment** | F(3.77,254.47)=3.11, **p=.018** |

Every other term retains the same conclusion as the raw-RT model. The three-way interaction is the one place the two scales disagree: non-significant (marginal) on raw ms (p=.061), significant on log RT (p=.018). This analysis showed nearly identical picture to raw RT, however the Experiment × Gaze × Action interaction was here indicated as significant rather than marginal.

## Standardized GC-effect analysis (Cohen's d)

To maximally rule out that the raw-ms result understates a real effect because the three response modalities carry different amounts of baseline RT noise, the congruency effect was also computed per subject as a standardized effect (Cohen's d: each subject's own congruent- and incongruent-trial RTs pooled into one SD, then the mean difference divided by it), and submitted to the same 3 (condition) × 3 (Experiment) design.

| Term | Result |
|---|---|
| d (main effect) | F(1.84,247.74)=31.15, p<.001, η²ₚ=.187 |
| **d × Experiment** | F(3.67,247.74)=3.28, **p=.015**, η²ₚ=.046 |
| Experiment (collapsed across condition) | 0/3 pairwise comparisons significant (all p_holm≥.925) |

We observed a significant interaction between Cohens' d and experiment (modality of the movement): F(3.67,247.74)=3.28, p=.015, η²ₚ=.046. The simple main effect of experiment showed significant difference only in inconsistent condition (F(2,135)=4.25, p=.016), where as no significant difference was observed for consistent (F(2,135)=1.51, p=.226) or control (F(2,135)=0.52, p=.597) condition.
Unlike with raw RT the post hoc on cohens d on effect size per action condition show significant difference between all conditions (not just active vs. control one): consistent vs. inconsistent d=0.341, t(135)=6.91, p_holm<.001; consistent vs. control d=0.101, t(135)=2.67, p_holm=.009; inconsistent vs. control d=-0.240, t(135)=-5.31, p_holm<.001.

The interaction post hoc - the cohens d x experiment - showed 12 of 36 comparisons as significant, and every one of them involves the inconsistent condition compared against either consistent or control — in some cases within the same experiment, in some cases across experiments:

- Exp1 cons vs. Exp2 incons: d=0.874, t(135)=3.95, p_holm=.003
- Exp1 cons vs. Exp3 incons: d=1.051, t(135)=4.77, p_holm<.001
- Exp2 cons vs. Exp2 incons: d=1.160, t(135)=4.96, p_holm<.001
- Exp2 cons vs. Exp3 incons: d=1.337, t(135)=6.27, p_holm<.001
- Exp3 cons vs. Exp2 incons: d=1.179, t(135)=5.51, p_holm<.001
- Exp3 cons vs. Exp3 incons: d=1.356, t(135)=5.86, p_holm<.001
- Exp2 incons vs. Exp1 ctrl: d=-0.804, t(135)=-3.68, p_holm=.009
- Exp2 incons vs. Exp2 ctrl: d=-0.691, t(135)=-3.22, p_holm=.040
- Exp2 incons vs. Exp3 ctrl: d=-0.873, t(135)=-4.13, p_holm=.002
- Exp3 incons vs. Exp1 ctrl: d=-0.981, t(135)=-4.51, p_holm<.001
- Exp3 incons vs. Exp2 ctrl: d=-0.868, t(135)=-4.12, p_holm=.002
- Exp3 incons vs. Exp3 ctrl: d=-1.050, t(135)=-4.95, p_holm<.001

Consistent and control cells never differ significantly from each other, and no same-condition comparison across experiments (e.g., Experiment 1 consistent vs. Experiment 2 consistent) reaches significance. The inconsistent condition accounts for the entire pattern. (Worth noting: Experiment 1's own inconsistent cell never differs significantly from anything at all in this table — not its own consistent/control cells, nor the other experiments' inconsistent cells. The whole 12-pair pattern is carried by Experiments 2 and 3.)

## GC-effect ANOVA (raw ms, no covariate)

Finaly, to be able to observe those differences in ms the same 3 (condition) × 3 (Experiment) design was run directly on the raw-ms difference score, rather than the standardized version - raw GC-effect size.

As in the previous analysis, using raw data, it indicated a marginal p value for the interaction and a significant simple effect of effect size.

| Term | Result |
|---|---|
| Effect size (main effect) | F(1.88,253.86)=22.09, p<.001, η²ₚ=.141 |
| **Effect size × Experiment** | F(3.76,253.86)=2.33, **p=.060** (marginal) |
| Experiment (collapsed across condition) | F(2,135)=0.07, p=.936, η²ₚ<.001 |

The simple main effect of GC effect size over experiment showed that the difference in effect size was not significant in exp 1 (F(2,80)=1.10, p=.338), however was significant in exp 2 (F(2,94)=12.19, p<.001) and exp 3 (F(2,96)=13.49, p<.001), being the more movement involving ones. A post hoc on effect size showed again significant difference between the inconsistent condition and the other 2: consistent vs. inconsistent MD=28.90 ms, t(135)=5.92, p_holm<.001; inconsistent vs. control MD=-21.55 ms, t(135)=-4.59, p_holm<.001. Unlike the Cohen's d version, consistent and control did not differ significantly from each other here (MD=7.34 ms, t(135)=1.87, p_holm=.063, n.s.).

A Post hoc on interaction (36 pairwise comparisons, now verified against the JASP output): 10 of 36 significant, all involving the inconsistent condition — the same pattern as the Cohen's d version, with two fewer significant pairs (10 vs. 12), consistent with the raw-ms scale being the least sensitive of the two methods used here. The two pairs that drop out relative to Cohen's d are Exp2 inconsistent vs. Exp1 control (MD=-25.01 ms, t(135)=-3.14, p_holm=.054, trend only) and Exp2 inconsistent vs. Exp2 control (MD=-23.71 ms, t(135)=-2.99, p_holm=.084, n.s.) — both were significant on the Cohen's d scale.

## A follow-up one-way ANOVA

Since all of the above analyses were showing that the difference was coming from the inconsistent condition, a follow-up one-way ANOVA was run on each subject's per-condition gaze-effect score (log RT congruent − incongruent), with Experiment as the between-subjects factor, tested separately within each action condition. This localizes the three-way interaction specifically to the inconsistent condition (F(2,135)=3.55, p=.031); consistent and control show no reliable cross-experiment difference.

The Holm-corrected pairwise breakdown of this contrast shows Experiment 1 differs from Experiment 3 (p=.042); Experiment 1 vs. Experiment 2 is a trend only (p=.068); Experiment 2 and Experiment 3 do not differ (p=.725). Confirming the influence of movement modality in the inconsistent condition.

## Summary across methods

| Method | Gaze × Action × Experiment (or equivalent) |
|---|---|
| Mixed ANOVA, raw RT | p=.061 (marginal) |
| Mixed ANOVA, log RT | p=.018 |
| Cohen's d × Experiment | p=.015 |
| GC-effect ANOVA (raw ms) × Experiment | p=.060 (marginal) |
| GC-effect ANOVA (log) × Experiment | p=.016 |

Raw-ms computations land marginal every time they are tried (p=.060–.061); any computation correcting for scale — log transform or per-subject noise-standardization — lands clearly significant every time (p=.015–.018), regardless of whether it is run as the full factorial design or as a collapsed single-DV model. Where the effect is localized (inconsistent condition specifically) and which pairwise experiment comparison survives correction (Experiment 1 vs. Experiment 3) is consistent across every method that reaches significance.
