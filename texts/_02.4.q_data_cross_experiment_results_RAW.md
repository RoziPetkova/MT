# Cross-Experiment Summary — Questionnaire (SoA Ratings) Results

All three experiments used the same 3 (Question type: caused, controlled, predicted) × 3 (Action Consistency: consistent, inconsistent, control) repeated-measures ANOVA on the questionnaire ratings, with Greenhouse–Geisser correction applied to all within-subject terms with more than one degree of freedom. Final samples: Experiment 1, *N* = 41; Experiment 2, *N* = 48; Experiment 3, *N* = 49.

**Descriptives**

Across all three experiments, ratings followed the same broad ordering: highest in the active conditions, lowest in the control condition. The three items did not always agree on their internal ordering, however — in Experiment 1, causation was rated highest overall, followed by prediction and then control; in Experiments 2 and 3, prediction was instead rated numerically highest, with causation and control close together at the bottom.

Because each experiment is an independent sample with no participant rating more than one response modality, ratings cannot be compared across experiments on their own terms as a within-subject repeated measure — but the absolute rating level for a given question and condition can still be compared *between* experiments directly, using `Experiment` as a between-subjects factor. Nine one-way ANOVAs (one per question × condition combination, on `mixed_anova_all_experiments.csv`) tested exactly this:

| | Caused | Controlled | Predicted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consistent | *F*(2,135)=25.98, *p*\<.001, η²ₚ=.278 | *F*(2,135)=5.53, *p*=.005, η²ₚ=.076 | *F*(2,135)=10.45, *p*\<.001, η²ₚ=.134 |
| Inconsistent | *F*(2,135)=7.99, *p*=.001, η²ₚ=.106 | *F*(2,135)=3.35, *p*=.038, η²ₚ=.047 | *F*(2,135)=3.59, *p*=.030, η²ₚ=.051 |
| Control | *F*(2,135)=1.92, *p*=.150 (n.s.), η²ₚ=.028 | *F*(2,135)=5.46, *p*=.005, η²ₚ=.075 | *F*(2,135)=0.93, *p*=.397 (n.s.), η²ₚ=.014 |

Six of the nine cells differed significantly by experiment. Descriptively, Experiment 1 rated all three questions higher than Experiments 2 and 3 in the consistent condition (e.g., caused: 5.70 vs. 3.88 vs. 3.56), while the direction reversed for the *controlled* item in the control condition, where Experiment 1 rated it lower than Experiments 2 and 3 (1.52 vs. 2.30 vs. 2.29). Experiment 2 and Experiment 3 sat close to one another almost everywhere — Holm-corrected pairwise comparisons found only one nominal Experiment 2-vs-3 difference across all nine cells, and it did not survive correction — while Experiment 1 differed from at least one of the other two in every significant cell. Because the direction of this difference flips depending on condition rather than running uniformly higher or lower, it is unlikely to reflect a simple response-style bias (e.g., Experiment 1's participants using higher numbers throughout); it more plausibly reflects a genuine difference tied to whatever separates Experiment 1 procedurally from Experiments 2 and 3.

**Analyses**

The omnibus effects within each experiment are summarized below:

| Effect | Experiment 1 | Experiment 2 | Experiment 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Action Consistency | *F*(1.84, 73.74) = 82.56, *p* \< .001, η²ₚ = .674 | *F*(1.73, 81.10) = 30.57, *p* \< .001, η²ₚ = .394 | *F*(1.64, 78.71) = 29.13, *p* \< .001, η²ₚ = .378 |
| Question type | *F*(1.70, 68.10) = 20.61, *p* \< .001, η²ₚ = .340 | *F*(1.64, 77.01) = 3.52, *p* = .043, η²ₚ = .070 | *F*(1.87, 89.85) = 1.93, *p* = .153, η²ₚ = .039 (n.s.) |
| Action Consistency × Question type | *F*(2.32, 92.86) = 26.26, *p* \< .001, η²ₚ = .396 | *F*(2.18, 102.31) = 11.14, *p* \< .001, η²ₚ = .192 | *F*(2.41, 115.46) = 6.27, *p* = .001, η²ₚ = .116 |

The Action Consistency main effect — the manipulation check — held in all three experiments regardless of response modality, though its size roughly halved from Experiment 1 to Experiment 2 and then held steady into Experiment 3 (.674 → .394 → .378). The Question type main effect, by contrast, declined in a clean, monotonic step from strongly significant (Experiment 1) to weakly significant (Experiment 2) to non-significant (Experiment 3): the three items became progressively less distinguishable from one another, collapsed across condition, as the direction-selection response became more embodied. The interaction between the two factors also shrank monotonically (.396 → .192 → .116) but remained significant in every experiment.

Decomposing the interaction in both directions replicated across all three experiments. Examined within each item, the simple main effect of Action Consistency was significant everywhere, with an identical ranking of item sensitivity in all three experiments — causation and control showing the largest effects, prediction consistently the smallest (Experiment 1: *F*(2,80) = 89.23, 55.84, 46.32; Experiment 2: *F*(2,94) = 33.06, 23.40, 8.32; Experiment 3: *F*(2,96) = 26.33, 25.86, 13.44, for causation, control, and prediction respectively). Examined the other way — the simple main effect of Question type within each condition — produced the cleanest monotonic trend in the dataset: in the two active conditions, the three items' ability to differentiate from one another declined step by step across the three experiments (consistent condition: *F*(2,78) = 14.50, *p* \< .001 in Experiment 1; *F*(2,94) = 2.95, *p* = .057 in Experiment 2; *F*(2,96) = 0.24, *p* = .788 in Experiment 3; inconsistent condition: *F*(2,78) = 28.74, *p* \< .001; *F*(2,94) = 4.23, *p* = .017; *F*(2,96) = 0.32, *p* = .725), while remaining significant throughout in the control condition (*F*(2,78) = 23.05; *F*(2,94) = 13.17; *F*(2,96) = 11.82, all *p* \< .001). The more embodied the direction-selection response, the less participants separated causation from control from prediction while actively doing something — even though they continued to separate them clearly when passive.

Because control is the one condition where this question-type effect stayed significant in all three experiments, its pairwise structure is the only one that can be decomposed with the same justification in every experiment. In the control condition, prediction was rated significantly higher than both causation and control in all three experiments, and causation and control never differed significantly from one another (caused vs. predicted: *d* = −0.63, −0.62, −0.55 in Experiments 1–3, all *p* \< .001; controlled vs. predicted: *d* = −0.77, −0.52, −0.39, *p* \< .05 in all three; caused vs. controlled: *p* ≥ .075 in all three, never significant beyond a marginal effect in Experiment 1). This is the single most consistent finding in the questionnaire data across the three experiments.

Finally, a single mixed ANOVA combining all three experiments directly (Question type × Action Consistency × Experiment, all nine cells in one model, *N* = 138) confirmed that this pattern is not merely a coincidence across nine separate one-way tests, but a formally significant three-way effect in its own right:

| Term | Result |
|---|---|
| Question type | *F*(2,270)=15.30, *p*\<.001, η²ₚ=.102 |
| Question type × Experiment | *F*(4,270)=6.39, *p*\<.001, η²ₚ=.086 |
| Action consistency | *F*(2,270)=145.15, *p*\<.001, η²ₚ=.518 |
| Action consistency × Experiment | *F*(4,270)=18.26, *p*\<.001, η²ₚ=.213 |
| Question type × Action consistency | *F*(4,270)=44.25, *p*\<.001, η²ₚ=.247 |
| **Question type × Action consistency × Experiment** | **F(8,540)=5.61, *p*\<.001, η²ₚ=.077** |
| Experiment (between) | *F*(2,135)=2.41, *p*=.093 (n.s.) |

Every term in the model was significant except the plain between-subjects effect of Experiment — the three experiments did not differ in overall rating level, only in the shape of the Question type × Action Consistency profile. The nine one-way ANOVAs and the two simple-main-effects decompositions above are therefore properly read as a decomposition of this single, formally confirmed three-way interaction, not as a loose family of unrelated tests.
